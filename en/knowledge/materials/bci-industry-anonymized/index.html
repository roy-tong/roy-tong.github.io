Consumer-level brain imaging: scene, paid evidence and verification sequence

The technical constraints, consumer scenarios, professional procurement and paid evidence of wearable brain imaging are disassembled, and the conclusion of the desensitization research is given what to verify first and what cannot be launched.

Research problems

A new type of wearable brain imaging device hopes to enter sports training, physical and mental health and daily state management. The technical ability seems clear, but the product problem is not automatically established:

  • What kind of users need additional brain status information at what time?
  • Why are the existing load, heart rate, sleep and subjective scales not enough?
  • Who is willing to pay for "an extra layer of information", an individual, a coach, a club or a research institution?
  • From measuring the signal to changing a decision, what verification is missing in the middle?

This page only discusses these issues and does not evaluate any specific equipment or institutions.

The three-tier market can't be mixed.

Public materials often put three types of budgets together:

  1. Scientific research and clinical equipment budget: The buyer is a laboratory, hospital or research platform, with a long decision-making cycle, and attaches importance to parameters, methods and compliance;
  2. Professional sports and institutional service budget: The buyer is a club, training center or guarantee team, paying attention to on-site usability and decision-making gain;
  3. Budget for consumer health equipment: The buyer is an individual and pays attention to wearing, price, brand and instant feedback.

The existence of scientific research procurement does not mean that consumer demand is established; the willingness of consumers to try does not mean that institutions will purchase. The three layers of products, evidence and channels must be separated.

Chain of evidence

For consumer-level new products, the most common mistake is to use questionnaires to measure imagination. The order of more reliable evidence is:

Rank What do you need to see? What can be supported?
E0 Scene and activity context It can only show that the problem may occur.
E1 Identify tasks, pain points and expected results Hypothesis of supporting problems
E2 Alternative, detour, failure or cost It proves that the user has taken action on the problem.
E3 Real interaction and selection of candidates Support plan accepted
E4 Real purchase or transaction Support business behavior
E5 Continuous use, repurchase or expansion Support retention and long-term value

Different stages cannot replace each other. A large number of scenario discussions cannot be upgraded to purchase evidence; a trial cannot prove long-term use.

Technical constraints must be written into the product definition first.

Research after anonymization retains four types of hard constraints:

  • Signal quality and motion artifacts: whether the device can stably obtain available signals in real activities;
  • Wearing burden: added weight on the head, position stability and long-term comfort;
  • Information increment: compared with the existing indicators, whether the new signal changes the judgment;
  • Using links: whether preparation, calibration, waiting, interpretation and exception handling are simple enough.

If the device can only work in a static environment, the demand cannot be proved by the discussion volume of the dynamic scene. If incremental information cannot change the choice of the coach or user, stronger sensors will not automatically form product value.

The structure of paid evidence

Public research has not found that the consumer-grade whole machine has formed a stable scale of purchase. The more common payment subjects are:

  • Equipment procurement for research and educational institutions;
  • Project budget for professional training or health services;
  • Teams with clear research needs for specific abilities.

However, institutional procurement may also come from research budgets, policy projects or equipment updates, which is not equal to market-oriented product demand. For each piece of paid evidence, it should be answered: who paid, what was bought and delivered, whether the contract was renewed, and whether it can be copied in a similar institution.

The cheapest verification order

  1. Use existing indicators to complete the same task, and record which step the user cannot judge;
  2. Only add target signals, compare with tasks, and observe whether the decision-making has changed;
  3. Interview buyers, users and maintainers separately;
  4. Verify the attribution of the budget with real procurement or paid pilot;
  5. Only after the first three steps are established can the scene and sample be expanded.

This is cheaper than continuing to expand the public corpus, and it is also more able to prove falsifying in advance.
