Home quadriped robot: task, failure recovery and product line constraints

Starting from the family security, care, children and companionship scenes, distinguish between discussion clues and real use evidence, and give the restrained boundaries of the quadruped robot product line.

Suitable for answering

Can't be extrapeded

First judge: what the family needs is the task, not the form.

In public discussions, "wanting a robot" is often just a desire for convenience, companionship or safety, and is not bound to the quadrupedal form. Directly interpreting this desire as "the need for a family quadruped robot" will skip three questions:

  1. Whether the task is frequent, and why the existing equipment can't be completed;
  2. Who will take over after the failure, and how long will it take to recover;
  3. Whether users are willing to pay for equipment, maintenance and consumables continuously.

The advantages of the quadruped form are mobility, obstacle crossing and anthropomorphic interaction; the price is noise, safety, endurance, maintenance and family acceptance. The product must prove that these costs are worth it with specific tasks.

Four candidate scenes

Home security and inspection

The discussion volume is high, but fixed cameras, door magnets, alarms and sweeping robots have covered a large demand. The increment of the quadruped equipment should be answered: which positions can't be seen by the fixed camera, whether the mobile inspection really occurs, and who will deal with the abnormal confirmation.

Simply "can walk around and watch the house" is easy to become a demonstration function, rather than continuous use.

Home service for the elderly and the disabled

Real problems exist, but buyers, users and maintainers are often separated. What needs to be verified is not whether the elderly like robots, but whether the children are willing to buy them, whether the elderly continue to use them, and whether the caregivers undertake additional settings and rescue.

Fall detection, pick-up and emergency call may overlap with existing equipment. If the first appointment promises medical or care results, the evidence requirement will be much higher than that of companionship or inspection.

Children's safety and special needs

Emotional and companionship needs are real, but children's scenes have higher requirements for security, privacy, content and long-term interaction. Public discussion is more about wishes and imagination, and lacks continuous use of evidence.

Direct home robot discussion

The core insight is that what users expect is "roles", not a list of functions. Housekeeper, errand, caregiver and companion are different roles, corresponding to different authority, failure responsibility and business model.

Failure recovery is the main line.

The long tail of the family environment is much higher than the display environment. Stairs, carpets, pets, children, temporary displays and network interruptions will all cause failure.

Product evaluation should be recorded:

  • Can it recover by itself after being stuck?
  • When it can't be restored, how many steps does the user need to walk and how long does it take;
  • Whether remote takeover is available;
  • Whether the failure will affect the safety of family members;
  • The same recovery action occurs several times a week.

If the recovery cost is higher than the task saving, the mobility is a liability.

Suggestions for the first release scope

More restrained initials should meet:

  • A clear scenario, no promise of general housework;
  • A repeatable narrow task;
  • There is a clear failure stop and manual takeover;
  • Don't "accompany" to cover up the lack of value of the task;
  • Commercially, verify equipment, services and consumables separately, and do not treat the intention as income.

Don't write the three types of evidence in one sentence.

  • Public discussion can only provide scene clues;
  • The trial can verify the acceptance and operational burden;
  • Real purchase, continuous use and renewal support the business conclusion.
