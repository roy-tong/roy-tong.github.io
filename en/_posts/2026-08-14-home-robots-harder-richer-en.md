    <h1>When the robot enters the family, it passes the level of &quot;who will clean up the mess&quot; first.</h1>
    <p class="post-subtitle">It is more difficult in technology and may be richer in economy - but the labor saved must be combined with its new labor.</p>
    <div class="post-meta">
      <time datetime="2026-08-14T09:13:00+08:00">2026-08-14</time>
      <span>12 minutes to read</span>
      
      <a class="post-translation" href="/notes/home-robots-harder-richer/" hreflang="zh-CN">阅读中文版 →</a>

  <div class="prose">
    <p>Imagine a home robot that can put the cups on the table back into the cabinet.</p>

<p>When the demonstration is successful, the picture is complete: identify, grab, move, and put down. But at home, there are two paragraphs before and after this matter. Before it starts, does someone have to empty the desktop and open the cabinet door? The cup is not stable. Who will deal with it? Who will check and maintain it before the next use?</p>

<p>This is a hypothetical situation, not the test result of a certain product. However, it points out that the most easily missed account in the judgment of home robot products: the labor it saves must be counted together with its new labor. I call the latter &quot;the burden of aftercare&quot;.</p>

<p>I led the product work of a household consumer-grade security quadruped robot, which was on the table at that time. The task itself does not seem complicated: after the user leaves home, the robot patrols the house, goes to the scene to take a look when it finds abnormalities, sends the picture and status back to the mobile phone, and intervenes with light or sound if necessary. It&#x27;s much narrower than &quot;doing all the housework&quot;. But once it is put into the real family, the problem will get longer quickly--</p>

<p>Who defines the exception? Does the child get up in the middle of the night? Do you want to go if your pet knocks something down? When can I enter the bedroom? Do you still patrol when there is someone at home? The two family members gave the opposite instructions. Who did they listen to? The robot is stuck at the threshold. What can remote users do? If it makes a sound to scare the elderly, is the product a success or a failure?</p>

<p>These questions made me re-understand the word &quot;family scene&quot;. Family is not a scene, but a set of continuously changing environment, multi-person relationships and life rules. When the robot enters it, it faces not only perception, navigation and operation, but also authority, disturbance, responsibility and recovery.</p>

<p>This is also the contradiction of home robots: it is technically more difficult than the factory, but it may be more abundant than a traditional household appliance economically - hardware, subscription, service, security, care, all of which may become income. But behind each layer of value is a new layer of delivery responsibility. Let&#x27;s calculate the aftermath first, and then talk about enrichment.</p>

<h2 id="工厂可以改造环境家庭只能被适应">Factories can transform the environment, and families can only adapt to it.</h2>

<p>It is also difficult for robots in the factory, but the factory can transform the environment: re-paving the ground, reset the workstation, unify the materials, fix the path, add lights if the light is not enough, and change the fixture if the grip is unstable. Tasks, personnel and maintenance processes can be specified.</p>

<p>The family is just the opposite. The same cup is in the kitchen today and in the bedroom tomorrow; the clothes are soft, the transparent containers are difficult to identify, and toys and wires appear on the ground at any time; children, the elderly, visitors and pets will not act according to the robot&#x27;s instructions. Different families have different definitions of &quot;the room is cleaned up&quot;.</p>

<p>BEHAVIOR-1K made this long tail into a robot benchmark: the research team sorted out 1,000 daily activities from user surveys, put 50 types of scenarios and more than 9,000 objects, and the experimental conclusion was very restrained - these tasks often required long-term planning and complex operations, and advanced methods were still difficult to complete.<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09227">BEHAVIOR-1K</a></p>

<p>This set of numbers does not mean that a family needs a robot to do a thousand things. It shows that the long tail is not an occasional noise in the family, but the long tail is the family itself.</p>

<h2 id="家务不是一个任务">&quot;Housework&quot; is not a task.</h2>

<p>Industry discussions often put cleaning, cooking, nursing, companionship, security and carrying into the word &quot;housework&quot;. They happen in the same room, but not the same product task.</p>

<p>If there is a corner missing from the floor, the user can make up for it. Failure to take the glass may damage the items. A security misreport is boring, and the consequences of missing a real risk are completely different. Accompanying the robot answered a wrong sentence, missed the emergency abnormality with the nursing system, and is not at the same responsibility level.</p>

<p>Therefore, to judge the family task, you can&#x27;t just ask the robot &quot;can do it&quot;. You should ask three things: what the result the user wants, what will happen if you make a mistake, and whether you can come back safely after making a mistake. This directly changes the boundaries of the product - &quot;chatting with the elderly&quot; and &quot;reminding you to take medicine&quot; seem to be adjacent, and the latter has much higher requirements for accuracy, logs and responsibility; &quot;night patrol&quot; and &quot;go to the site for confirmation after finding abnormalities&quot; are also different. The former is a continuous autonomous behavior, and the latter can leave the final judgment to others.</p>

<p>The order is also determined from this: first define a result that can be accepted, and then decide how much movement, operation and autonomy is needed. It&#x27;s not to make a long list of abilities first, and then look for the needs for it.</p>

<h2 id="善后的账藏在成功率的分母里">The account of the aftermath is hidden in the denominator of the success rate.</h2>

<p>We are used to evaluating robots by the success rate of the task. But there is a problem with the denominator of success rate: where the task starts and ends, which determines what the number means.</p>

<p>If the environment is sorted out by people before each start, the test measures the performance in the scene after sorting out; if the robot fails and needs to be sent back to the starting point, but only the final completion is counted, the takeover cost will be omitted. This kind of deviation is especially easy to occur in the family, because there will always be someone to help: hand it over if the equipment is not enough, move it if it is stuck, and if you don&#x27;t understand the instructions, you can say it in another way. Every time it is a small action, and together it may become a long-term position.</p>

<p>The evaluation of the robot should not only ask how much it has done for others, but also ask who was forced to cooperate with it.</p>

<p>And there is a second problem with this account: the person who pays the bill is not necessarily the one who takes care of the aftermath. Family is not like a demand card with only one user - some people offer to buy, some people use it every day, and some people are responsible for charging, connecting to the Internet, cleaning and contacting customer service. The equipment bought for parents, the setting problem is handled by the children remotely; the daily maintenance of the new equipment that one person likes may fall on another person. Therefore, there is an illusion at the product level: the buyer is satisfied, the product also has sales, but the total burden on the family may not be reduced. Save ten minutes of A and add fifteen minutes of B, which cannot be said that the family has obtained net income - unless the family is willing to accept this division of labor, which itself should be confirmed in product research and cannot be acquiesced.</p>

<p>Maintenance itself is not a unique defect of robots. The key is proportion and predictability. The maintenance table listed by iRobot for the Roomba s series covers the care of filters, brushes, sensors and other components, which shows that mature household appliances do need maintenance, but the failure rate of all robots cannot be calculated based on this.<a href="https://homesupport.irobot.com/articles/en_US/Knowledge/20966">Official maintenance instructions</a>What is really difficult is not regular cleaning - maintenance that can be arranged once a week and random help every day are not the same burden in life. Only the accumulated minutes will miss this difference.</p>

<h2 id="恢复能力要单独评审">The recovery ability should be evaluated separately.</h2>

<p>When the technical parameters fall into the family, it is another matter: the navigation accuracy is whether it will hit the child and be trapped between the legs of the chair; the robotic arm control will be whether it will break the cup and pinch the finger; the number of decibels of noise determines whether it can work at night; the battery life determines whether it is lying on the charging base when the user really needs it.</p>

<p>And the most easily underestimated is the recovery ability. It is obvious progress for the laboratory to raise the success rate of tasks from 80% to 90%; what home users see is that there is still one in ten times to clean up the mess, and failure is often not &quot;run again&quot; - the robot may be entangled in clothes, lose the map, stop at the door, or pick up something that should not be moved.</p>

<p>A study of 10,072 consumer reviews of home robots divided failures into three categories: technology, interaction and service. What is more harmful to user evaluation is whether the task is completed and whether the equipment can continue to work in the face of changes and failures.<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.03287">Domestic Robot Failures</a></p>

<p>So I will take out the &quot;recoverability&quot; separately for evaluation: whether the robot knows that it has failed; whether the user can understand where it stops; whether the equipment can safely stop, return or ask for help; whether an ordinary family can recover by itself without the need for engineers to remotely trouble. There is no full-time operation and maintenance in the family - it is still a high-burden product to complete nine times a day and trouble every two days.</p>

<p>The action boundary after failure should be written into the product definition: you can try again, change the method, ask for help, or stop. Endless retry is a waste of time, and wrong retry may increase the loss; asking for help too early to return the work that could have been completed to others; stopping must make the family understand - where the task is stopped, whether the scene is safe, and what to do next. In multi-person families, help cannot be sent to the nearest person by default: the product needs to let the family know who is responsible for what kind of problem, and also allows certain tasks not to be clearly accepted.</p>

<h2 id="多人家庭的权限不是一个管理员账号">The permission of a multi-person family is not an administrator account.</h2>

<p>Home robots also have a problem that ordinary apps rarely encounter: who is the user? The person who buys the device, the person who gets along with it every day, the person who is scanned by the camera, and the person who views the screen remotely may not be the same person. Children, the elderly, nannies and visitors will not be affected by robots because they don&#x27;t have accounts.</p>

<p>In 2026, a multi-family participatory design study visited 15 families: participants wanted more direct data control, clear status notifications, and personalized rules that could change with members, and were also worried about whether manufacturers could properly handle personal data in a multi-person environment.<a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16975">Multi-person Households’ Privacy Design Preferences</a></p>

<p>This kind of requirement can&#x27;t just be stuffed into the setting page. It will enter the underlying architecture: who can let the robot into which room; who can see real-time images, history and event logs; how the people on the scene know when filming, recording or remote takeover; which data is only processed locally; what rules to deal with when member instructions conflict; how to downgrade the security ability when the network is disconnected or the account fails; whether emergency stop is always available.</p>

<p>There are four things: the right to purchase, the right to use, the right to be perceived and the responsibility of care. Set the buyer as a super administrator, which can&#x27;t solve the family consensus.</p>

<h2 id="经济上更丰富责任上也更重">It is more economically rich and more responsible.</h2>

<p>After the above accounts, it is the turn of the attractive side of the home robot: it can participate in labor, information, security, care and family coordination at the same time, and the economic structure is much richer than selling hardware at one time.</p>

<p>There has been an exploration of this combination on the market: 1X provides NEO with an Early Access buyout plan of $20,000, and also lists a subscription of $499/month; for complex tasks that robots will not do for the time being, users can make an appointment for remote manual guidance.<a href="https://www.1x.tech/order">1X NEO Official Order Page</a>This case can only prove that the enterprise is trying &quot;hardware + subscription + manual service&quot;, but it cannot prove that the model has been run. On the contrary, it is very blunt: when the robot&#x27;s ability is insufficient, the manual can make up for the closed loop, but the manual brings scheduling, privacy, response speed and unit economic problems at the same time.</p>

<p>The back of the income stack is the responsibility stack. Selling hardware should be responsible for quality, warranty and recall; selling subscriptions should continuously deliver perceptible value - &quot;hardware gross profit is not enough, then rely on subscription&quot; is not the answer. First of all, it is necessary to explain what the result of the continuous delivery of subscriptions is; providing remote labor should bear the responsibility of service network and privacy; entering security and care, it is necessary to deal with false reports, missed reports and upgrade mechanisms.</p>

<h2 id="现有市场证明的只有窄任务">The existing market proves that there are only narrow tasks.</h2>

<p>According to World Robotics 2025 of the International Federation of Robotics, in its supplier sample, the sales of consumer-grade service robots in 2024 will be about 20.1 million units, of which home mission robots account for 97%; only 536 home care robots were registered. IFR also reminds that these data come from supplier samples and should not be extrapelled to the sales volume of the whole industry.<a href="https://ifr.org/img/worldrobotics/Executive_Summary_WR_2025_Service_Robots.pdf">IFR Executive Summary</a></p>

<p>Sweeping and mowing have found high-frequency, visible and clear tasks, so they have run to tens of millions of units; care and general operations have to enter a more unstructured environment and face higher safety requirements, so there are still hundreds of units. The sales of narrow tasks can prove that families are willing to pay for robots, but it cannot prove that general-purpose humanoid robots are about to replicate the same maturity. For each cross-level task, technology and responsibility need to be re-verified.</p>

<p>The evidence on the discussion side should also be used with caution. The home robot corpus I am sorting out has checked a total of 208,755 records, of which Reddit accounts for about 88.15%, with limited coverage; the lesson of the local AI corpus group is also valid here - users complain that doing housework explains a problem, users buy sweepers to explain that a certain plan is supported by behavior, stitching the two into &quot;users are willing to buy general home robots&quot;, and what is missing in the middle is evidence.<a href="/en/knowledge/data/">Data and boundaries</a></p>

<h2 id="怎么设计一场有用的家庭测试">How to design a useful family test</h2>

<p>First, record the original practice of the family: who completes the task, how often it happens, where the time and attention are used, and which places are good enough. Then introduce the robot to retain the complete process of task preparation, automatic execution and aftermath; in addition to normal success, take the initiative to arrange interruptions and marginal situations within the allowed range, and observe how the equipment stops, interprets and restores - do not remove difficult failures from the statistics for the sake of beautiful data.</p>

<p>The test should continue beyond the initial sense of novelty, during which both the buyer and the person who actually uses and maintains it should be asked; if a member replaces the equipment for a long time, this is the product cost that needs to be explained. In the end, it may be found that a plan with fewer functions but lighter aftercare is more popular than a plan with a wider range of capabilities but frequent help; it may also be found that users are willing to undertake additional maintenance for a high-value capability. Both results need real task support.</p>

<p>Now to judge whether a kind of home robot is worth doing, I will ask seven questions first:</p>

<ol>
  <li>What is the specific result of the user&#x27;s purchase, not how many actions the robot will do?</li>
  <li>How often does this result occur naturally in ordinary families?</li>
  <li>Compared with cameras, mobile phones, smart speakers or manual services, what are the irreplaceable capabilities of robots?</li>
  <li>What is the most common failure, and what is the cost of one failure?</li>
  <li>After a mistake, can ordinary families recover safely and easily?</li>
  <li>Does the main family member understand when it perceives it, how it acts, and is willing to accept it?</li>
  <li>Is the new delivery responsibility of the enterprise still cost-effective for each additional level of income?</li>
</ol>

<p>If most of the questions are not answered clearly, humanoid, wheeled, quadruped or flying are not the key. The most attractive thing about home robots is that they seem to be able to do anything, and the trap is also here: they can do everything, which often means that nothing has been defined to be delivered.</p>

<p>There is no dispute that home robots need stronger movement ability. But on the day it enters life, the product responsibility will extend to the failure of the action - so I hope that the next demonstration will not cut it out when the cup is crooked. Then look at what it does and what people need to do.</p>
